The program

A practical approach backed by aerial monitoring

Built on more than two decades of practical livestock management and precision aerial work, our methodology shows that well-managed grazing can improve plantation outcomes while delivering meaningful agricultural, environmental and economic benefits.

Livestock and carbon farming do not have to compete for the same land. Carbon Grazing Australia creates a framework where both can work together to improve long-term landscape performance.

Methodology

Every grazing program is individually designed

Our objective is straightforward: deliver environmental results you can verify, while maintaining the highest standards of animal welfare and practical farm management.

  • Property assessment and planning
  • Plantation mapping and risk assessment
  • Grazing management plans
  • Stock selection and welfare management
  • Rotational grazing strategies
  • Dry matter and fuel load management
  • Weed suppression
  • Tree protection protocols
  • Ongoing technical support
  • Verification and reporting

Technology

Powered by technology. Guided by experience.

Farming knowledge decides how the grazing runs; aerial monitoring proves it's working. Drone imagery and LiDAR track pasture, trees and grazing impact at every stage of the project.

The result for project owners is simple: clear evidence that the grazing program is doing its job — without compromising tree establishment or pasture health.

  • High-resolution aerial mapping
  • LiDAR terrain and vegetation modelling
  • Dry matter estimation
  • Vegetation health monitoring
  • Tree survivability assessments
  • Grazing impact analysis
  • Before-and-after imagery
  • GIS mapping and reporting

Delivery

Supporting carbon projects across Australia

A professional grazing management service that complements existing land management practices — tailored to each site, because no two landscapes, grazing systems or carbon projects are the same.

  • Grazing strategy development
  • Demonstration site establishment
  • Supply of trained livestock
  • Livestock management and husbandry
  • On-property grazing supervision
  • Collaboration with farm managers and project owners
  • Ongoing monitoring and reporting
  • Seasonal program reviews
