The program

A practical, data-driven approach

Built on more than two decades of practical livestock management and modern precision technology, our methodology demonstrates that well-managed grazing can become a valuable management tool, improving plantation outcomes while delivering meaningful agricultural, environmental and economic benefits.

Rather than viewing livestock and carbon farming as competing land uses, Carbon Grazing Australia creates a framework where both can work together to improve long-term landscape performance.

Methodology

Every grazing program is individually designed

Our objective is simple: deliver measurable environmental outcomes while maintaining the highest standards of animal welfare and practical farm management.

  • Property assessment and planning
  • Plantation mapping and risk assessment
  • Grazing management plans
  • Stock selection and welfare management
  • Rotational grazing strategies
  • Dry matter and fuel load management
  • Weed suppression
  • Tree protection protocols
  • Ongoing technical support
  • Verification and reporting

Technology

Powered by technology. Guided by experience.

Carbon Grazing Australia combines practical farming knowledge with advanced aerial intelligence to monitor and verify grazing outcomes throughout every stage of a project, using drone technology, LiDAR, multispectral imagery and artificial intelligence.

This provides project owners with objective evidence that grazing programs are achieving their intended outcomes while protecting plantation assets.

  • High-resolution aerial mapping
  • LiDAR terrain and vegetation modelling
  • Dry matter estimation
  • Vegetation health monitoring
  • Tree survivability assessments
  • Grazing impact analysis
  • Before-and-after imagery
  • GIS mapping and reporting

Delivery

Supporting carbon projects across Australia

A professional grazing management service that complements existing land management practices, tailored to the individual site and recognising that no two landscapes, grazing systems or carbon projects are the same.

  • Grazing strategy development
  • Demonstration site establishment
  • Supply of trained livestock
  • Livestock management and husbandry
  • On-property grazing supervision
  • Collaboration with farm managers and project owners
  • Ongoing monitoring and reporting
  • Seasonal program reviews
